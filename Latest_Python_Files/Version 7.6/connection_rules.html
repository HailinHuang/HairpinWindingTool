01 · Phase map

Phase belts follow electrical position—not a fixed slot-label cycle

This ring is one concrete V7.6 example generated from the current phase-map function: 12 slots, 4 poles, 3 phases, 2 layers, q=1 and zero layer shifts. It shows layer 1 only. Change the slot/pole combination, q or layer shift and the phase/sign pattern may change.

For P poles, θe = (P/2)θm; slot electrical pitch is 360°(P/2)/S. Equal phase populations do not prove balanced phase EMF or equal parallel-branch EMF.

02 · Polarity

Read +/− from the assigned phase map

For the illustrated q=1, two-pole-pair example, successive slot records alternate phase sign: U+, V−, W+, U−, V+, W−. That sequence describes this phase map; it is not a universal rule that every connection edge or every Pattern branch must alternate.

A branch must follow its actual ordered conductor path and signed records. Keep phase membership, path direction and winding EMF as separate checks.

03 · Auto Configure priority gate

Preserve the neutral reference welds, and keep each layer pair direction-uniform

Before symmetry, electrical checks and objective ranking, Auto compares the candidate's physical weld-edge multiset with the neutral reference. Counts matter, and edges are branch-agnostic so an insertion-side tail exchange may move a preserved weld to another branch.

For each cross-layer weld, direction is measured lower-to-higher layer after correcting the configured phase shifts. Same-layer welds count toward physical edge preservation but are omitted from cross-layer direction grouping. The examples isolate common failure symptoms; a real candidate may violate both clauses.

04 · Rule map

Separate structural, electrical, Pattern and manufacturing checks

A pass at one level is not a substitute for the next. Which connection pitches and terminal routes are legal depends on the selected Pattern and divider route.

05 · Further reading

Sources and scope

These sources provide winding-design, connection and manufacturing context. They do not define the V7.6 Auto weld-side contract; that contract is a tool-specific search invariant.

  1. Zou et al. (2022). “A Comprehensive Design Guideline of Hairpin Windings for High Power Density Electric Vehicle Traction Motors.” IEEE Transactions on Transportation Electrification, 8(3), 3578–3593. Covers layout choices including transposition, parallel branches, terminals, phase shift and pitch, and reports prototype manufacturability validation. doi:10.1109/TTE.2022.3149786
  2. Dannier et al. (2022). “Hairpin Winding Technology for Electric Traction Motors: Design, Prototyping, and Connection Rules.” International Conference on Electrical Machines (ICEM), 1170–1175. Discusses winding connection choices and impedance balance among parallel paths. doi:10.1109/ICEM51905.2022.9910851
  3. Pasang et al. (2025). “Recent Advancements in Hairpin Welding: Manufacturing and Properties.” International Journal of the Society of Materials Engineering for Resources, 27(1), Article 665. Reviews hairpin assembly and welding process/property considerations. doi:10.5188/ijsmer.665
  4. IEC 60034-8:2026. Rotating electrical machines — Part 8: Terminal markings and direction of rotation. Scope is connection-point identification, terminal marking and rotation-direction relationships; it is not cited here as an internal hairpin-topology or weld-side rule. IEC Webstore record
